# ADR-0046: DShot Frames Follow The Control Loop

Status: accepted, pending target validation; amends the cadence in ADR-0039

Each accepted control-loop output starts one DShot frame set. Motor commands
and bidirectional eRPM (ADR-0045) therefore run at the loop rate, 2 kHz on both
flight boards. The 2 ms service period passed on one control output in four,
and it would cap eRPM at 500 Hz.

A bidirectional frame set takes about 85-100 us at DShot600: about 27 us of
command, about 30 us of turnaround, and about 28 us of reply. That is under a
fifth of the 500 us loop period.

The DShot service stays the only frame starter (W1). It is released by each
command `actuator_output` accepts, instead of by its own 2 ms timer. The 1 kHz
SysTick monotonic cannot schedule a 500 us period; releasing from the control
path avoids needing a finer timebase. The frame is not ordered against the
IMU's SPI transfer, which follows the sensor's own data-ready interrupt;
ADR-0045 records what that means for its receive window. A disarm releases it
too, so stop frames go out at once rather than at the next
fallback release.

Without accepted commands, for example while disarmed, during guarded idle, or
if the control loop stops, a millisecond-timer release keeps frames going every
2 ms. That release enforces the lease as today. The 20 ms command lease, the
stop-and-disarm on lease expiry, and the 1 ms frame-set fault bound are
unchanged. Guarded-idle qualification (ADR-0048) therefore sees replies at
500 Hz or more.

This is a DShot timing change (W6). It needs logic-analyzer measurement of
frame spacing and jitter, the props-off DShot and arming checks, and a flight
re-gate.
