# ADR-0061: The Border Roles Are Safety-Critical And Run Below The Core

Status: accepted, not yet implemented; amends ADR-0054, ADR-0056, ADR-0060

Decided by the user on 2026-10-20.

**The border.** The coordinator, the observer and the Health Manager live on
the border of the safety-critical core. All three are safety-critical, and
all three run below the control path and the safety master and above every
non-critical task, so a border role that hangs or faults cannot stall the
core. A border role's failure raises "land immediately" (ADR-0062), never a
hard fault. This amends ADR-0054's priority rule, which put the observer
with the non-critical tasks below every safety-critical one.

**The coordinator** is ADR-0056's. Its main purpose is to stop unprivileged
tasks from stalling the system by spamming or stalling the safety master:
each requester has its own queue and the coordinator takes a bounded number
of requests per run.

**The observer becomes safety-critical** (amends ADR-0056). The Health
Manager reads the ledger, and a safety-critical crate never depends on a
non-critical one. Its producers' never-block rule stands.

**The Health Manager** aggregates data below a hard fault and turns it into
actionable items in the safety master's fault classes (ADR-0062). It may
decide that a combination of data is a hard fault, and relays it to the
safety master. It is not a primary detector: a dead or stale IMU, RC loss, a
stopped control loop or an actuator fault is detected by the consuming task's
gate (ADR-0055) and reported straight to the safety master. The Health
Manager may use the same data for an aggregated fault. It reads its data from
the observer's ledger. Each escalation rule is a requirement with its own
evidence, since a false hard fault ends a flight.

**Heartbeats.** Each border role sends a heartbeat (sequence, timestamp) to
the safety master over a `state` link, beating even when idle. The safety
master checks their ages in its periodic check (ADR-0063); one older than its
role's limit raises "land immediately" with the role's id.

**A stalled ledger.** The OSD and telemetry show a warning when the ledger
topics they read stop advancing (each topic carries a sequence). That covers
an observer stuck waiting or no longer publishing, the one observer failure
that leaves the OSD running: a panic stops the whole MCU, and a busy hang
starves the OSD, which runs below the border roles. "Land immediately" from a
hung coordinator or Health Manager reaches the pilot through the observer.

**One crate** (amends ADR-0060). The three roles share one safety-critical
`ferrowasp-border` crate, one module each; `ferrowasp-safety` keeps the
safety master, arming and actuator authority. The ledger's topic types live in
`ferrowasp-boundary-contracts`, so the OSD and telemetry depend on the
contracts, not on the border crate. A new crate is made only when its bench
or flight evidence must survive changes to its neighbour, or for a standalone
driver or protocol; otherwise code is a module. The boundary that matters is
border roles versus the safety master; between the border roles evidence is
mostly unit and SITL, so sharing a crate costs nothing.

Deferred: making the safety master domain-agnostic, with the RC, IMU and
actuator specifics moved out of it.
